\subsection{Local Noise-Floor Adaptive Spectral Thresholding}
Load changes the SES floor in both directions: at $0$~HP the impacts are weak
and a fixed threshold set for normal running sits above the fault peaks, while
at $3$~HP friction raises the whole floor and the same threshold is crossed by
noise in dozens of bins. No single fixed value satisfies both cases. A CFAR
rule~\cite{richards} slides a window along the SES and estimates the local floor
from the bins around the test bin, so the comparison is always made against the
floor that is actually there, with no training data.

For a test bin $k$, the $N_G=4$ bins on each side are kept out of the estimate,
which covers the four-bin Hann mainlobe so a fault peak cannot enter its own
estimate. The $N_R=25$ bins beyond each guard band form the reference window
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{W}_k=\big\{\text{SES}[m]\;\big|\;&
  m\in[k-N_G-N_R,\,k-N_G-1]\\
  &\cup[k+N_G+1,\,k+N_G+N_R]\big\}.
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:window}
\end{equation}
Two estimators are compared on $\mathcal{W}_k$.

\subsubsection{Cell-Averaging CFAR}
Cell averaging takes the mean of the window, as a moving average would:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mu}[k]=\frac{1}{2N_R}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{W}_k}x,
  \qquad T_{\text{CFAR}}[k]=\alpha\,\hat{\mu}[k],
  \label{eq:cacfar}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha=3.5$. This works while the floor changes slowly, but it fails when
a harmonic enters the window: a shaft harmonic at $1\times$ or $2\times$ RPM or a
nearby fault harmonic pulls the mean up, raising the threshold exactly where it
should stay low, because a single large bin is enough to move a mean. This
harmonic target masking is why the next estimator exists.

\subsubsection{Proposed Order-Statistic Median--MAD Threshold}
The proposed rule uses order statistics instead of the mean:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \hat{m}[k] &= \operatorname{median}\left(\mathcal{W}_k\right),\\
  \text{MAD}[k] &= \operatorname{median}\left(\left|\mathcal{W}_k-\hat{m}[k]\right|\right).
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:medmad}
\end{equation}
The factor $1.4826$ is the nominal scale that makes the MAD agree with the
standard deviation of a normal distribution, $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{local}}[k]
=1.4826\,\text{MAD}[k]$, used here as a fixed scale factor rather than an
unbiased Gaussian $\sigma$ estimate, because the SES floor is not Gaussian. The
threshold is
\begin{equation}
  T_{\text{MAD}}[k]=\hat{m}[k]+\gamma\left(1.4826\,\text{MAD}[k]\right),
  \label{eq:tmad}
\end{equation}
with $\gamma=7.0$: a larger $\gamma$ lowers the false-alarm rate and a smaller
one raises it. The median has a $50\%$ breakdown point~\cite{huber}, so up to
half the window can be contaminated before $T_{\text{MAD}}$ moves, which lets
the threshold follow the floor from $0$~HP to $3$~HP.

\subsubsection{Slip-Tolerant Harmonic Comb}
Heavy radial load also makes the rolling elements skid by $1$--$2\%$, so the
measured fault frequency is not the kinematic value and the detector searches a
band, not a single bin,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{B}_h=\left[hf_{\text{fault}}(1-\delta_{\text{slip}}),\;
  hf_{\text{fault}}(1+\delta_{\text{slip}})\right],
  \label{eq:comb}
\end{equation}
for each fault frequency ($f_{\text{BPFO}}$, $f_{\text{BPFI}}$ or
$f_{\text{BSF}}$) and harmonic order $h=1,2,3$. The half-width
$\delta_{\text{slip}}=1.5\%$ excludes neighbouring orders while covering the
slip. A harmonic is present when
\begin{equation}
  \max_{f_k\in\mathcal{B}_h}\left(\text{SES}[k]-T_{\text{MAD}}[k]\right) > 0.
  \label{eq:decision}
\end{equation}

The exceedance rate this rule produces is not a thermal-noise false-alarm
probability: on a bearing SES the eligible non-harmonic bins hold unmasked
shaft harmonics and modulation sidebands, so the measured rate counts the
discrete interference lines the harmonic mask did not cover.
