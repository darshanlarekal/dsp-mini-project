\emph{1) Light load, $0$~HP.} Under no load the impacts are weak and the raw
record sits below the noise, at Raw SNR $=-7.82$~dB. The static threshold
misses every harmonic. The fault lines never reach the fixed $0.055$ level, so
the detection rate is zero.

\emph{2) Full load, $3$~HP.} At $3$~HP the vibration level rises through shaft
deflection and electromagnetic hum. The static rule still finds the harmonics
(TDR $=100\%$), but the raised floor pushes $224.6$ noise bins across the same
line. Detecting every harmonic counts for little when hundreds of bins
false-alarm alongside it.

\emph{3) CA-CFAR masking.} Cell averaging follows the load, and its false-alarm
count stays in single digits. Its weak point is the fault itself. Strong
harmonics inside the reference window raise the local threshold, and that
threshold then hides the neighbouring and higher-order harmonics. At $0$~HP the
rule loses one harmonic of three, which drops the detection rate to $66.7\%$.

\emph{4) Median--MAD.} The proposed detector holds $100\%$ at every load, with
at most $0.6$ false-alarm bins. Neither the median nor the MAD moves unless
half the window is contaminated, so the tall impulses in $\mathcal{W}_k$
cannot lift the threshold that is meant to expose them. The rule is the one
defined in Section~II-D, Eq.~\eqref{eq:tmad}.
