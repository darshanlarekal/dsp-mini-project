\emph{1) Light loading failure ($0$~HP).} Under no-load conditions the impacts
are weak and the raw record sits below the noise (Raw SNR $=-7.82$~dB). The
static threshold misses every harmonic: its detection rate is zero, because the
fault lines never reach the fixed $0.055$ level.

\emph{2) Heavy loading degradation ($3$~HP).} At full load the vibration energy
rises through shaft deflection and electromagnetic hum. The static threshold
still finds the harmonics (TDR $=100\%$), but the raised floor drives
$224.6$ noise bins past the same line. A detector that false-alarms on hundreds
of bins is not usable, whatever its detection rate says.

\emph{3) CA-CFAR target masking.} Cell averaging reacts to the load, and its
false-alarm count stays in single digits. Its failure is different: strong
harmonics inside the reference window inflate the local threshold, so the
neighbouring and higher-order harmonics are suppressed. At $0$~HP this drops the
detection rate to $66.7\%$.

\emph{4) Median--MAD robustness.} The proposed detector holds a $100\%$
detection rate at every load with at most $0.6$ false-alarm bins. The median and
the MAD both break down only when half the window is contaminated, so the tall
impulses in $\mathcal{W}_k$ cannot lift the threshold that is meant to expose
them. The rule itself is the one defined in Section~II-D,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:tmad}.
