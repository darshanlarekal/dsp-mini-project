\subsection{Summary of Diagnostic Achievements}
The end-to-end run, from the raw accelerometer channel to the thresholded
envelope spectrum, gives five results.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The Fast Kurtogram found the resonance band by itself: $f_c=5181.8$~Hz
        with $545.5$~Hz of bandwidth on the $0$~HP outer-race record, with the
        search restricted to $[1000~\text{Hz}, f_s/2-150~\text{Hz}]$ and no
        manual tuning of the filter.
  \item On the outer-race $7$~mil record both adaptive rules recovered the full
        BPFO comb in every segment at every load ($100\%$ TDR), whereas the
        $0$~HP-calibrated fixed level averaged $40$--$80\%$ with spreads up to
        $55$ points.
  \item Across all $36$ CWRU fault records the adaptive rules raised the mean
        TDR from $40.9\%$ (static) to $57.8\%$ (CA-CFAR) and $56.3\%$
        (Median--MAD) and cut the mean FAR from $10.7\%$ to $5.0\%$ and
        $4.4\%$; on the MFPT fault records both reached $100\%$ TDR at FAR
        $1.7$--$2.2\%$ while the CWRU-calibrated level flooded $89$--$100\%$ of
        the bins, and on the healthy baselines the adaptive rules stayed at
        $0.0$--$1.9\%$ crossings.
  \item Zero-phase Butterworth SOS filtering with coherent-gain calibrated
        Hilbert envelope analysis added $13.4$--$16.4$~dB of spectral SNR on
        the CWRU outer-race record and $16.1$~dB on the MFPT outer-race
        records, while the MFPT inner-race records already carried sharp raw
        harmonics and gained nothing ($-0.25$~dB).
  \item With Q$2.14$ coefficients and a Q$1.15$ bus, the fixed-point cascade
        keeps $r_{\max,q}=0.96262<1$ and reaches $70.05$~dB passband SQNR,
        against the $98.08$~dB ideal for $16$~bits; the gap is set by the
        coefficient rounding of the high-$Q$ denominator, not by the signal
        bus.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Embedded Hardware Feasibility and Real-Time Budget}
The same pipeline was timed and re-run in fixed point, to check its fit on an
edge device.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Memory.} A $2.0$~s block ($N=24\,000$ samples) at $16$~bits per
        sample needs $48$~kB of SRAM and the $32\,768$-bin complex FFT buffer
        at $16$~bits per component needs $128$~kB, a working set of about
        $176$~kB that fits an STM32H7 Cortex-M7 part.
  \item \emph{Execution latency.} The interpreted Python (CPython/Colab)
        pipeline takes $0.29$--$0.50$~s for a $2.0$~s CWRU window, so the duty
        cycle is
\begin{equation}
  \eta = \frac{T_{\text{compute}}}{T_{\text{acquire}}}
  \approx \frac{0.29\text{--}0.50~\text{s}}{2.0~\text{s}}
  = 15\text{--}25\%.
  \label{eq:duty}
\end{equation}
        Even uncompiled the chain runs several times faster than real time, so
        the processor idles for three quarters of the timeline or more; this is
        a software timing, not an embedded benchmark. The MFPT records, sampled
        at $48.828$~kHz, take $0.67$--$0.92$~s per $2.0$~s segment
        ($34$--$46\%$ duty).
  \item \emph{Fixed-point suitability.} The $70.05$~dB Q$1.15$/Q$2.14$ SQNR
        sits $28$~dB below the $98.08$~dB signal-bus ideal because the high-$Q$
        denominator coefficients are rounded to $16$~bits. The harmonic
        decision survives (Section~IV-F), so integer MAC units can run the
        filter while the envelope FFT stage keeps a wider internal word
        length.
\end{enumerate}
