\subsection{Summary of Diagnostic Achievements}
Five results came out of the end-to-end run on the CWRU and MFPT records, which
starts at the raw accelerometer channel and ends at a thresholded envelope
spectrum.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The Fast Kurtogram found the resonance band by itself: $f_c=5181.8$~Hz
        with $545.5$~Hz of bandwidth on the $0$~HP outer-race record, with the
        search restricted to $[1000~\text{Hz}, f_s/2-150~\text{Hz}]$ and no
        manual tuning of the filter.
  \item On the outer-race $7$~mil record, CA-CFAR and Median--MAD both
        recovered the full BPFO harmonic comb in every segment at every load
        ($100\%$ TDR), whereas the fixed level calibrated at $0$~HP averaged
        only $40$--$80\%$ with spreads of up to $55$ points.
  \item Across all $36$ CWRU fault records the adaptive rules improved the mean
        TDR from $40.9\%$ (static) to $57.8\%$ (CA-CFAR) and $56.3\%$
        (Median--MAD), and cut the mean FAR from $10.7\%$ to $5.0\%$ and
        $4.5\%$; on the MFPT outer- and inner-race records both reached
        $100\%$ TDR at FAR $1.7$--$2.2\%$, while the CWRU-calibrated static
        level flooded $89$--$100\%$ of the bins. On the healthy baselines the
        adaptive rules stayed at $0.0$--$1.9\%$ crossings.
  \item Zero-phase Butterworth SOS filtering, followed by coherent-gain
        calibrated Hilbert envelope analysis, added $13.4$--$16.4$~dB of
        spectral SNR on the CWRU outer-race record and $16.1$~dB on the MFPT
        outer-race records; the MFPT inner-race records already carried sharp
        raw harmonics and gained nothing ($-0.25$~dB).
  \item With Q$2.14$ coefficients and a Q$1.15$ bus, the fixed-point cascade
        keeps $r_{\max,q}=0.96262<1$ and reaches $70.05$~dB passband SQNR,
        against the $98.08$~dB ideal for $16$~bits; the gap is set by the
        coefficient rounding of the high-$Q$ denominator, not by the signal
        bus.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Embedded Hardware Feasibility and Real-Time Budget}
The same pipeline was timed and re-run in fixed point, to check its fit on an
edge device.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Memory.} A $2.0$~s block ($N=24\,000$ samples) at $16$~bits per
        sample needs $48$~kB of SRAM, and the $32\,768$-bin complex FFT buffer
        at $16$~bits per component needs $128$~kB, for a working set of about
        $176$~kB. That fits an STM32H7 Cortex-M7 part, which carries several
        hundred kilobytes of SRAM.
  \item \emph{Execution latency.} The interpreted Python (CPython/Colab)
        pipeline takes $0.35$--$0.42$~s for a $2.0$~s CWRU window, so the duty
        cycle is
\begin{equation}
  \eta = \frac{T_{\text{compute}}}{T_{\text{acquire}}}
  \approx \frac{0.35\text{--}0.42~\text{s}}{2.0~\text{s}}
  = 18\text{--}21\%.
  \label{eq:duty}
\end{equation}
        Even without compilation the chain is about five times faster than
        real time, and the processor idles for about four fifths of the
        timeline. The figure is a software timing, not an embedded benchmark.
        The MFPT records, sampled at $48.828$~kHz, take about $0.81$--$0.90$~s
        per $2.0$~s segment ($41$--$45\%$ duty).
  \item \emph{Fixed-point suitability.} At $70.05$~dB the Q$1.15$/Q$2.14$
        SQNR is $28$~dB below the $98.08$~dB signal-bus ideal, because the
        high-$Q$ denominator coefficients are rounded to $16$ bits. The
        harmonic decision survives (Section~IV-F), so integer MAC units can run
        the filter, while the envelope FFT stage keeps a wider internal word
        length.
\end{enumerate}
