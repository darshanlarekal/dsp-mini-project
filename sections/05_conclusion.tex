\subsection{Summary of Diagnostic Achievements}
Four results came out of the end-to-end run on the CWRU and MFPT records, which
starts at the raw accelerometer channel and ends at a thresholded envelope
spectrum.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The Fast Kurtogram found the resonance band by itself: $f_c=5181.8$~Hz
        with $545.5$~Hz of bandwidth on the $0$~HP outer-race record, and no
        manual tuning of the filter.
  \item CA-CFAR and Median--MAD both recovered the BPFO harmonic comb at every
        load, with mean detection rates of $100$, $60$, $86.7$ and $100\%$
        from no load to full load.
  \item Zero-phase Butterworth SOS filtering, followed by coherent-gain
        calibrated Hilbert envelope analysis, gave between $19.2$ and
        $26.7$~dB of spectral SNR improvement across the load sweep.
  \item At $\gamma=7.0$ the Median--MAD rule matched CA-CFAR's detection
        rate and crossed fewer non-harmonic bins at every load
        ($4.3$--$6.5\%$ against $5.2$--$7.0\%$); the fixed $0.055$ threshold
        did not transfer across loads.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Embedded Hardware Feasibility and Real-Time Budget}
The same pipeline was timed and re-run in fixed point, to check its fit on an
edge device.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Memory.} A $2.0$~s block ($N=24\,000$ samples) at $16$~bits per
        sample needs $48$~kB of SRAM. That fits an ARM Cortex-M4 or M7 part
        such as the STM32F4/H7, which carries $512$~kB.
  \item \emph{Execution latency.} The interpreted Python (CPython/Colab)
        pipeline takes between $0.35$ and $0.45$~s for a $2.0$~s window, so
        the duty cycle is
\begin{equation}
  \eta = \frac{T_{\text{compute}}}{T_{\text{acquire}}}
  \approx \frac{0.35\text{--}0.45~\text{s}}{2.0~\text{s}}
  = 17\text{--}23\%.
  \label{eq:duty}
\end{equation}
        Even without compilation the chain is $4.4$--$5.7$ times faster than
        real time, and the processor idles for more than three quarters of the
        timeline. The figure is a software timing, not an embedded benchmark.
        The MFPT records, sampled at $48.828$~kHz, take about $0.71$~s per
        $2.0$~s segment.
  \item \emph{Fixed-point suitability.} At $29.76$~dB the Q$1.15$ SQNR is far
        below the $98.08$~dB ideal, because the simulation quantizes both
        coefficients and the signal bus on a record with a crest factor near
        five. The harmonic decision survives (Section~IV-F), so integer MAC
        units can run the filter, while the envelope FFT stage keeps a wider
        internal word length.
\end{enumerate}
