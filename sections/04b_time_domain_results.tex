\subsection{Time-Domain Resonance Demodulation Progression}
Fig.~\ref{fig:time_progression} follows one segment through three stages, over
$100$~ms ($1200$ samples at $12$~kHz).

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{Fig2_Time_Domain_Progression.png}
\caption{Time-domain signal progression across a $100$~ms segment:
(a) pre-processed zero-mean vibration signal $x_0[n]$ dominated by low-frequency
motor hum and broadband noise ($K = 2.23$), (b) Kurtosis-guided zero-phase SOS
bandpass filtered output $y_{\text{BP}}[n]$ across $[2000, 4000]$~Hz isolating
high-frequency resonant impact bursts ($K = 4.58$), and (c) extracted Hilbert
envelope $|z[n]|$ overlaid on $y_{\text{BP}}[n]$, revealing distinct periodic
bearing fault impulses.}
\label{fig:time_progression}
\end{figure}

In Fig.~\ref{fig:time_progression}(a) the zero-mean record is dominated by
low-frequency shaft and motor oscillation, with broadband noise on top. At
$107.36$~Hz the $100$~ms window holds about eleven impacts, yet the impacts are
buried. The kurtosis is $2.23$, below the Gaussian value of $3.0$,
and no individual strike can be picked out by eye.

In Fig.~\ref{fig:time_progression}(b) the signal has passed through the
Kurtosis-guided zero-phase SOS filter for $[2000, 4000]$~Hz. The motor hum is
gone. What remains is a train of underdamped bursts, one per impact on the outer
race. Stripping the low-frequency energy and keeping the phase aligned raises
the kurtosis from $2.23$ to $4.58$, more than double.

Fig.~\ref{fig:time_progression}(c) overlays the Hilbert envelope $|z[n]|$ on the
filtered waveform. The envelope follows the top of each burst, so the $3000$~Hz
carrier becomes a sequence of positive pulses. Their spacing is
$\Delta t \approx 9.31$~ms, which is $1/107.36$~Hz, the outer-race fault
frequency. The spacing of the envelope peaks, not their height, carries the
fault information here. That envelope is what the SES stage consumes.
