\subsection{Time-Domain Resonance Demodulation Progression}
Fig.~\ref{fig:time_progression} demonstrates the step-by-step time-domain signal
progression over a $100$~ms window ($1200$ samples at $f_s = 12$~kHz).

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{Fig2_Time_Domain_Progression.png}
\caption{Time-domain signal progression across a $100$~ms segment:
(a) pre-processed zero-mean vibration signal $x_0[n]$ dominated by low-frequency
motor hum and broadband noise ($K = 2.23$), (b) Kurtosis-guided zero-phase SOS
bandpass filtered output $y_{\text{BP}}[n]$ across $[2000, 4000]$~Hz isolating
high-frequency resonant impact bursts ($K = 4.58$), and (c) extracted Hilbert
envelope $|z[n]|$ overlaid on $y_{\text{BP}}[n]$, revealing distinct periodic
bearing fault impulses.}
\label{fig:time_progression}
\end{figure}

In Fig.~\ref{fig:time_progression}(a), the pre-processed vibration signal
$x_0[n]$ is heavily dominated by high-amplitude, low-frequency sinusoidal
oscillations originating from shaft rotation and structural motor hum, alongside
additive broadband noise. Because the continuous low-frequency interference
masks the short-duration bearing impacts, the waveform exhibits a low Kurtosis
of $K = 2.23$ (below the Gaussian baseline of $3.0$), and individual fault
strikes cannot be visually distinguished.

In Fig.~\ref{fig:time_progression}(b), passing $x_0[n]$ through the
Kurtosis-guided zero-phase Cascade SOS Butterworth bandpass filter
($[2000, 4000]$~Hz) completely eliminates the low-frequency motor roar. The
resulting bandpass signal $y_{\text{BP}}[n]$ reveals a clean train of
high-frequency, underdamped ringing bursts excited each time a rolling element
strikes the outer-race defect. By stripping away the stationary low-frequency
energy and preserving phase alignment via zero-phase filtering, the time-domain
Kurtosis more than doubles from $K = 2.23$ to $K = 4.58$.

Finally, Fig.~\ref{fig:time_progression}(c) overlays the magnitude of the complex
analytic signal (the Hilbert envelope $|z[n]|$) directly onto the bandpass
waveform $y_{\text{BP}}[n]$. The envelope smoothly traces the outer amplitude
profile of each high-frequency resonant burst, converting the $3000$~Hz carrier
oscillations into sharp, positive-valued periodic pulses spaced at intervals of
$\Delta t \approx 9.31$~ms (corresponding to the outer-race fault frequency
$f_{\text{BPFO}} = 107.36$~Hz). This high-contrast envelope signal provides an
ideal input for subsequent Squared Envelope Spectrum (SES) transformation and
adaptive spectral thresholding.
