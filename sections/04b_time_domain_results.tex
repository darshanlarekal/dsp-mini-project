\subsection{Time-Domain Resonance Demodulation Progression}
Fig.~\ref{fig:time_progression} follows one segment through three stages, over
$100$~ms ($1200$ samples at $12$~kHz).

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{Fig2_Time_Domain_Progression.png}
\caption{Time-domain signal progression across a $100$~ms segment of the
$0$~HP outer-race record: (a) pre-processed zero-mean vibration signal $x_0[n]$
with periodic impact bursts ($K = 7.56$), (b) Kurtosis-guided zero-phase SOS
bandpass filtered output $y_{\text{BP}}[n]$ across $[4909, 5455]$~Hz isolating
the resonance that carries the impacts ($K = 7.81$), and (c) extracted Hilbert
envelope $|z[n]|$ overlaid on $y_{\text{BP}}[n]$, revealing the periodic
bearing fault impulses.}
\label{fig:time_progression}
\end{figure}

In Fig.~\ref{fig:time_progression}(a) the zero-mean record already shows the
impact train. At $107.36$~Hz the $100$~ms window holds about eleven bursts,
each an underdamped ringing of the structural resonance, spaced by roughly
$9.3$~ms. The signal is impulsive: its kurtosis is $7.56$, well above the
Gaussian value of $3.0$, because the impacts populate the tails of the
amplitude distribution.

In Fig.~\ref{fig:time_progression}(b) the signal has passed through the
Kurtosis-guided zero-phase SOS filter for $[4909, 5455]$~Hz. The low-frequency
shaft and motor content is removed and the resonance bursts remain, one per
impact on the outer race. The kurtosis moves from $7.56$ to $7.81$: the band
concentrates the impulsive energy, but the raw record is already impulsive, so
this statistic changes little. The band choice still matters for the envelope,
because it removes the low-frequency floor that would otherwise dominate the
demodulated spectrum.

Fig.~\ref{fig:time_progression}(c) overlays the Hilbert envelope $|z[n]|$ on the
filtered waveform. The envelope follows the top of each burst, so the
$5.2$~kHz carrier becomes a sequence of positive pulses. Their spacing is
$\Delta t \approx 9.31$~ms, which is $1/107.36$~Hz, the outer-race fault
frequency. The spacing of the envelope peaks, not their height, carries the
fault information here. That envelope is what the SES stage consumes.
