\subsection{Time-Domain Resonance Demodulation Progression}
Fig.~\ref{fig:time_progression} follows one segment through three stages, over
$100$~ms ($1200$ samples at $12$~kHz).

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\columnwidth]{Fig2_Time_Domain_Progression.png}
\caption{Time-domain signal progression across a $100$~ms segment of the
$0$~HP outer-race record: (a) pre-processed zero-mean vibration signal $x_0[n]$
with periodic impact bursts ($K = 7.56$), (b) Kurtosis-guided zero-phase SOS
bandpass filtered output $y_{\text{BP}}[n]$ across $[4909, 5455]$~Hz isolating
the resonance that carries the impacts ($K = 7.81$), and (c) extracted Hilbert
envelope $|z[n]|$ overlaid on $y_{\text{BP}}[n]$, revealing the periodic
bearing fault impulses.}
\label{fig:time_progression}
\end{figure}

In Fig.~\ref{fig:time_progression}(a) the zero-mean record already shows the
impact train: at $107.36$~Hz the $100$~ms window holds about eleven bursts, each
an underdamped ringing of the structural resonance spaced by roughly $9.3$~ms.
Its kurtosis is $7.56$, well above the Gaussian value of $3.0$, because the
impacts populate the tails of the amplitude distribution.

In Fig.~\ref{fig:time_progression}(b) the Kurtosis-guided zero-phase SOS filter
for $[4909, 5455]$~Hz removes the low-frequency shaft and motor content and
leaves one resonance burst per impact on the outer race. The kurtosis moves from
$7.56$ to $7.81$ only, since the raw record is already impulsive, but the band
choice still matters for the envelope: it removes the low-frequency floor that
would otherwise dominate the demodulated spectrum.

Fig.~\ref{fig:time_progression}(c) overlays the Hilbert envelope $|z[n]|$,
which follows the top of each burst and turns the $5.2$~kHz carrier into a train
of positive pulses spaced by $\Delta t \approx 9.31$~ms, that is $1/107.36$~Hz.
The spacing of the envelope peaks, not their height, carries the fault
information here, and this envelope is what the SES stage consumes.
